# Build a branded renderer, let AI assist

There is room for an automated thumbnail maker in the Quicktricks admin, and no vendor sells the version we need. No LMS, course platform or Indian coaching platform found in this research ships a tool that renders **branded, text-bearing, bilingual thumbnails in bulk from a fixed template**. Where course platforms offer AI images at all, they make generic art from a prompt, one image at a time. Video hosts treat a thumbnail as a pointer to a frame. Standalone "AI thumbnail makers" are YouTube clickbait tools with no API and no layout control. The key technical finding sets the design: **diffusion models still misspell Devanagari**, and the one independent Hindi evaluation found systematic errors in Gemini's output. A tool that draws "अधीनस्थ न्यायालय" as generated pixels would put typos in front of students. The recommended build is therefore a **deterministic HTML/CSS template rendered by headless Chromium**, which shapes Hindi correctly and reproduces the existing navy/red house style. It costs about $0 per image and takes a few days of engineering. It can backfill the ~1,551 "No Image" classes and give every new class a banner at creation. Generative AI belongs at the edges, as an optional text-free topic illustration composited into a fixed slot, at roughly $25–$100 for the whole backfill. Our existing 6,140 thumbnails already follow one template, so the job is mainly template rendering, with AI as an optional extra.

## Course platforms stop at generic art or a stock-photo picker

The mainstream creator platforms mostly ship nothing. Kajabi's own help page for its AI assistant lists "Images and graphics — No" ([Kajabi help](https://help.kajabi.com/hc/en-us/articles/12529965897115)). Teachable's AI writes sections and lessons, while the thumbnail stays a manual 1024×576 upload ([Teachable help](https://intercom.help/teachableknowledgebase/en/articles/11682451-create-and-set-up-your-course)). Docebo, Circle, TalentLMS and Heights fill the gap with **stock pickers that search Unsplash** and similar libraries, not with generation ([Docebo thumbnail doc](https://documentation.kw.com/html/_sources/kellercloud/connect/training/docebo/add-thumbnail.rst.txt); [Circle update](https://community.circle.so/c/product-updates/new-customization-options-for-topic-cover-images); [Heights blog](https://www.heightsplatform.com/blog/does-thinkific-have-ai-features-thinkific-ai-tools-reviewed-a-better-alternative)). Thinkific is unresolved: its help centre documents only a manual image upload, and a claim that its AI generator makes a "cover image" traces to a press release that could not be fetched ([Thinkific product image](https://support.thinkific.com/hc/en-us/articles/360035064553-Edit-Your-Product-Image-Description)).

The few platforms that do generate images make illustrative art from a prompt. **LightspeedVT** has a "Generate Image" button for course and category thumbnails. It uses the title and description as the default prompt, offers four style options and returns **two candidates per request** for the author to pick from ([LightspeedVT support](https://support.lightspeedvt.com/docs/create-thumbnails-with-the-ai-image-generator)). That is the cleanest UX reference found. **Teachfloor** is the only platform documented to generate covers for individual lessons and modules, still one prompt at a time ([Teachfloor help](https://support.teachfloor.com/en/articles/10568571-introducing-teachai-ai-powered-features-overview)). LearnWorlds lists image generation as one bullet in its AI course-creator suite ([LearnWorlds](https://www.learnworlds.com/ai-course-creator)). Absorb's 2026 releases add "AI-selected" images inside generated course pages, which are content images rather than thumbnails ([Absorb June 2026 release](https://support.absorblms.com/hc/en-us/articles/52142186383251-June-2026-Standalone-Release-Image-Updates-for-AI-Block-Pages-in-Absorb-Create)). None of these vendors documents a bulk run across a catalogue, a brand template, a logo slot or exact title typesetting. None makes the feature a headline either. That suggests generic AI art never became a strong selling point for course creators.

The Indian coaching platforms show even less. Graphy markets AI for outlines, sales and tutoring, and live-session thumbnails are added by hand ([Kripesh Adwani review](https://kripeshadwani.com/graphy-review/)). Edmingle markets an "AI Companion" with no image generation ([Edmingle blog](https://www.edmingle.com/blog/classplus-alternatives/)). Targeted searches for Classplus, Teachmint, Exly and AppX turned up nothing. This is absence of evidence from public pages, not proof. AppX publishes no product docs, and the in-house tooling at Physics Wallah, Testbook and Adda247 is not public. On present evidence, though, a Devanagari-capable, brand-locked banner generator would have no direct competitor among Indian LMS vendors.

The realistic substitute today is Canva. Its official LMS integrations target Google Classroom, Canvas, Schoology and Teams, not creator or coaching platforms ([Canva LMS integrations](https://www.canva.com/education/lms-integrations/)). In practice, institutes design in Canva and upload the PNG. Any in-dashboard tool has to beat the manual route of exporting titles to a CSV, running Canva Bulk Create and uploading the results.

## Video platforms automate the frame, creator tools automate the clickbait

Video infrastructure stores a thumbnail as a timestamp plus an image-transform URL. Cloudflare Stream exposes `thumbnailTimestampPct` and on-the-fly `?time=` thumbnails with no smart selection ([Cloudflare Stream docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/stream/viewing-videos/displaying-thumbnails/)). Bunny Stream offers a "Set Thumbnail" API and nothing AI-driven ([bunny.net docs](https://docs.bunny.net/api-reference/stream/manage-videos/set-thumbnail)). Kaltura defaults to the frame at the three-second mark ([Kaltura Knowledge Center](https://knowledge.kaltura.com/help/set-and-modify-thumbnails)). **Mux** is the exception. Its Robots workflow can pick the best frame and write it back to the asset's `thumbnail_time` ([Mux changelog](https://www.mux.com/docs/changelog/robots-best-thumbnail-in-video)). `find-best-thumbnails` was promoted to stable on **17 Sept 2026** ([Mux changelog](https://www.mux.com/docs/changelog/experimental-workflow-promotions-sept-2026)), and every plan gets **100,000 free Robots units a month** ([Mux pricing](https://www.mux.com/pricing.md)). Mux does not publish how it scores frames. No host found offers generative or branded-template thumbnails. Since our HLS sits on our own CDN rather than Mux, smart frame selection would have to be built.

YouTube now covers both generation and testing. **Test & Compare** runs up to three thumbnail or title variants and keeps the one with the **highest watch-time share, not the highest click-through rate** ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714?hl=en)). YouTube is deprecating its older Inspiration tab from August 2026 in favour of Ask Studio ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15575509?hl=en)). Ask Studio's thumbnail generator reportedly reads the transcript, takes a text prompt, outputs 1280×720 and labels results with SynthID and C2PA ([TubeBuddy blog](https://www.tubebuddy.com/blog/youtube-ai-thumbnail-generator/)). That detail comes from a single secondary source, and nothing found shows it handles brand templates or Hindi text. Creator editors follow the same generative route: **OpusClip Thumbnail runs on GPT-4o** and builds thumbnails from a video link ([OpusClip blog](https://www.opus.pro/blog/announcing-opusclip-thumbnail)), and VEED sells a prompt-to-thumbnail generator ([VEED](https://www.veed.io/tools/ai-image-generator/ai-thumbnail-maker)). Of these, only Canva and Adobe Express pair AI with templates and brand kits, the combination closest to our need.

| Technique | Who does it in 2026 | Fit for Quicktricks |
|---|---|---|
| Best-frame extraction | Mux Robots, Vimeo, Netflix AVA, Instagram/TikTok frame suggestions | Optional: a teacher-face frame from the lecture HLS |
| Template + text overlay | Canva, Adobe Express, manual design everywhere else | **Core requirement**: house style, logo, bilingual title |
| Fully generative | YouTube Ask Studio, OpusClip, VEED, Canva Magic Media | Text-free illustrations only; unsafe for Hindi titles |
| A/B or bandit testing | YouTube Test & Compare, Netflix contextual bandits | Later phase, judged on plays and completion |

The evidence that thumbnails matter comes mostly from platforms, not from education. Netflix found that artwork took **over 82% of members' focus while browsing**, at about 1.8 seconds per title. It also found that images with more than three people do much worse at small sizes ([Netflix](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/the-power-of-a-picture)). YouTube says **90% of its best-performing videos use custom thumbnails** and advises easy-to-read fonts ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en)), but that figure shows correlation, not cause. No study found measures the effect of thumbnails on lecture plays, in India or anywhere else. The strongest sourced case for this feature is therefore a "No Image" grey box in 1,551 slots, not a click-through gain.

## Standalone tools sell YouTube clickbait for $0.10 to $0.40 a thumbnail

The standalone category serves one creator making the next video. **Pikzels** charges $28–$56 a month on annual plans. A thumbnail costs 10–20 credits, and the selling points are face "Personas", recreating viral thumbnails from a pasted link, and a virality score. The pricing page mentions no API or batch mode ([Pikzels pricing](https://pikzels.com/pricing)). Its traction claims ("7.9B+ views generated") are its own. GetLatka estimates **$220K in 2025 revenue from a two-person team** ([GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/pikzels.com)), which makes Pikzels a fragile company to integrate with. 1of10 sells plans from $19 to $89 a month built on YouTube "outlier" data. vidIQ and TubeBuddy add generators to analytics suites, and ThumbnailTest sells A/B testing from $29 a month. Most of what ranks in search is vendors ranking themselves ([1of10 blog](https://1of10.com/blog/best-ai-thumbnail-generator/)). Their reported weaknesses are generic "AI faces", outputs that copy references closely and credits that run out fast ([wayin.ai](https://wayin.ai/blog/pikzels-alternative/)). They compete on face likeness, cloning viral thumbnails and click scoring, none of which helps here. A coaching LMS needs the opposite: a fixed layout, an exact title and thousands of images.

The products that do fit catalogue-scale work are **template-rendering APIs**. Bannerbear charges $49 a month for 1,000 renders and $149 for 10,000, about $0.015–$0.049 per image ([Bannerbear pricing](https://www.bannerbear.com/pricing/)). Templated.io and Placid compete on lower prices. Canva's own docs now say the **Connect Autofill API works for Canva Pro and Teams users**, not only Enterprise. Usage limits "will be introduced in the future" ([canva.dev Autofill](https://www.canva.dev/docs/connect/api-reference/autofills/)). Competitor pages still say Enterprise-only, so Canva's primary page is the one to trust. This is the strongest argument against building our own: a designer could own a Canva Brand Template while code fills in the title. Its costs are a dependency on a Canva account, rate limits still to come, and **no vendor statement that Autofill, Bannerbear or Placid shape Devanagari correctly**. Each would need a conjunct test before any commitment, and none removes the work of uploading to B2, writing the URL and adding admin UI.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Self-hosted HTML template, headless Chromium | ~$0 compute | ~$0 | Exact (browser shaping) | We maintain the template |
| Template + AI topic illustration ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)) | $0.017–$0.067 | ~$25–$100 | Exact | Provider integration, art cache |
| Bannerbear ([pricing](https://www.bannerbear.com/pricing/)) | $0.015–$0.049 | $49–$149 | Unverified | Subscription, vendor lock-in |

| Full AI image incl. title text | $0.04–$0.21 + retries | $62–$310+ | Errors measured on Gemini | OCR check + human review |
| Pikzels-style creator tools ([Pikzels](https://pikzels.com/pricing)) | ~$0.10–$0.40 | Manual, one by one | Unverified | No API, no fixed layout |

## Diffusion models still misspell Hindi, so code must draw the title

Generation is cheap: Gemini 3.1 Flash Image costs **$0.067 per 1K image, or $0.034 in batch**, and Nano Banana 2.1 costs $0.0168 in batch ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Spelling is the problem. The only independent human evaluation of Hindi text found used eight Hindi-literate raters. They scored GPT Image 1 at **4.58/5**, Gemini 3.1 Flash Image at **3.90/5** (it failed two of five prompts) and Gemini 2.5 Flash Image at 3.25/5. Errors included "राखी स्पेशल" rendered as "राकी एपेषाल" ([india-text-to-image-eval](https://github.com/AAAaMMbbaarr/india-text-to-image-eval)). The study used only five prompts. OpenAI says its newer models render Hindi better, but its own guide still calls text rendering imperfect and brand-element consistency a known challenge ([OpenAI image guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)). The ">95% Devanagari accuracy" figure circulating for GPT Image 2 is vendor copy with no stated method ([Segmind](https://www.segmind.com/models/gpt-image-2)). Our titles are short but dense with conjuncts (न्या, त्र, क्ष). Even 95% accuracy would leave about 75 misspelled banners in a 1,500-image backfill, and a misspelled Hindi title damages the credibility of an exam-prep brand.

Builders working in Hindi first have reached the same answer. One Hindi-first app stopped asking the model for text: "the engine draws the picture and the device's font draws the words". Its reasoning was that conjuncts and pre-base matras need glyph shaping that no diffusion model does ([navbharatai PR #3208](https://github.com/docasheesh-png/navbharatai/pull/3208)). That moves the risk to the renderer. **Satori and `@vercel/og`, the obvious choice in a Next.js stack, do not shape Devanagari.** Satori parses fonts with opentype.js and does not support ligatures or other OpenType features ([satori README](https://www.npmjs.com/package/satori?activeTab=readme)). A bug report shows Devanagari rendering "distorted and unreadable", and it was closed without a fix ([satori #516](https://github.com/vercel/satori/issues/516)). Renderers built on HarfBuzz handle the Indic shaping model correctly ([HarfBuzz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HarfBuzz)). These include headless Chromium and Pango-based canvases, and skia-canvas is a lighter in-process alternative ([skia-canvas](https://github.com/samizdatco/skia-canvas)). skia-canvas has not been tested on Devanagari, so it should go through the conjunct test before anyone chooses it.

## What this means for Quicktricks: render the house style, then add AI where it is safe

Our situation favours the template approach. All 6,140 existing banners follow **one house style**: a dark navy or red background, a large title (often in Devanagari), a small brand logo at the top and small footer text. Generative tools can only approximate that look, while a template reproduces it exactly. The gap is also concrete: about **1,551 classes show "No Image"** and have no source to recover a banner from, and admins create new classes every day. A deterministic renderer fixes the backlog in one batch and keeps the gap from reopening.

**Phase 1 is a self-hosted template renderer.** Rebuild the house style as an HTML/CSS template, with navy and red variants, the logo, a title block that adapts its size to English and Devanagari, and the footer. Bundle a Devanagari font such as Noto Sans Devanagari or Tiro Devanagari Hindi through `@font-face`. A Node worker renders the template with Puppeteer or Playwright at 16:9 (1280×720, YouTube's output size). It uploads the PNG or WebP to B2 under a content-hashed filename, so the Bunny CDN never serves a stale version. It then writes the URL to the class's Banner field. The Next.js admin can preview the same template live, which matches LightspeedVT's pattern of generating two options for the author to pick. The admin gets **Generate**, **Regenerate** and **Approve** buttons. New classes created without an upload get a generated banner automatically. Store a provenance flag (generated or uploaded) so that regenerating never overwrites a banner someone made by hand, and so that the copied AppX banners stay untouched. The backfill runs as a batch script over the 1,551 classes. It writes to a review gallery first, so a Hindi reader can check a sample before anything goes live. Before writing the template, render a test string covering क्ष, त्र, ज्ञ, श्र, द्ध, the short-i matra and the reph, plus real titles such as "अधीनस्थ न्यायालय", and compare the output with how Canva renders them. Marginal cost is effectively zero, and the build is a few days of work, not a project.

**Phase 2 adds AI only where a mistake is harmless.** A small LLM call can turn "गुप्त काल" into an English prompt for a text-free illustration, such as a Gupta-era coin or a gavel for the courts. The prompt should forbid text, logos and real people. The image model generates the illustration, which is composited into a fixed slot in the template and cached by topic, so repeated subjects reuse their art. At Gemini batch prices this costs about $25–$100 for the whole backfill ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). A second option is a teacher frame from the recorded HLS. ffmpeg's `thumbnail` and scene-change `select` filters can pull candidate frames cheaply ([FFmpeg filters](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html)), and a face-plus-sharpness heuristic can pick one. This is our own version of what Mux sells. Real frames carry no risk of a fake likeness. Whether teachers consent to appearing in thumbnails is a policy question to settle before shipping. Log the prompt and model ID with each generated asset. If the provider's moderation blocks a request, fall back to the plain template.

**Phase 3 is measurement, and only if Phase 1 proves useful.** YouTube judges thumbnail variants by watch time, and Netflix moved from A/B tests to bandits ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714?hl=en)). For a captive student audience, the equivalent is a simple two- or three-variant split judged on plays and completion. It is worth doing only once there is enough traffic per class to read a result.

The steelman for skipping the build is Canva: run Bulk Create once for the backfill, then have admins keep designing banners by hand. That clears the 1,551 slots without engineering. It leaves the daily chore in place and adds a Canva step to every class creation. Since the self-hosted renderer costs little more than wiring up Canva Autofill and has no usage limits on the way, building is the better choice. Fully generative thumbnails with AI-drawn Hindi titles should be rejected outright until an independent test shows Devanagari accuracy that holds up over thousands of images.

## Conclusion

The market question and the engineering question have the same answer. Vendors have avoided bulk, branded, bilingual thumbnails because the generative wave optimised for one creator's clickbait, while the hard part for an Indian coaching catalogue is correct typesetting at scale, which is old technology. The useful AI is narrow: choosing a frame, suggesting a topic illustration, translating a title. What makes the output correct is the font shaper, not the model. The way to fail is to reach for the trendy tool, whether a diffusion model writing Hindi or Satori because it ships with Next.js, and publish misspelled titles at scale.

There is a strategic upside too. A per-tenant template renderer is a small piece of infrastructure, and no Indian LMS vendor found offers anything like it. If the Hranker platform serves more institutes than Quicktricks, the same renderer with a different template per brand is a feature competitors lack. The open risks are the ones this research could not close. No one has independently tested Devanagari output from the template APIs or from GPT Image 2, nobody has measured whether better thumbnails raise lecture plays, and the Ask Studio and Thinkific claims rest on secondary sources. The Phase 1 conjunct test and a post-launch play-rate comparison would answer the two that matter most for us.
