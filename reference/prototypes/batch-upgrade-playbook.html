Where we are

How the upgrade works today

Live since 18 Sep, and tested on prod with a real ₹500 Razorpay payment on 19 Sep.

Set on the target batchAdmin ticks Upgrade on the batch to sell, picks the source batches, and enters one upgrade price.
Who qualifiesAn active paid order on any source batch, and the student does not already own the target.
Where it showsOnly on the Plans screen, as a lower price with the list price struck through.
What they getFull target validity. The source batch stays untouched. If the extension offer is cheaper, that one shows instead.

What is missing

Four gaps that cost us upgrades

Nobody tells the student

No push, no in-batch mention, no teacher shout-out. The offer waits on a screen most buyers never revisit.

One price, all year

A student on day 3 and one on day 300 see the same price, in exam-notification week and in the dead months alike.

No reason given

The card shows a price. It never says why this batch fits this student's exam or where their prep falls short.

We can't see the funnel

We record the upgrade order. We don't record who saw the offer or who tapped it, so we can't tell which pairs work.

Constraint to keep in mind: we can't ship a new app release right now, so the app only reads price and the buy/upgrade flags. The extension offer already works around this by putting its message in the plan name ("… : Extension (6 Months Validity)"). An upgrade can do the same. Each prototype below says whether it needs a release.

Pricing the upgrade

Credit for unused days, or a discount for existing students

Today every eligible student pays the same fixed upgrade price. The three rules below make the price depend on the student's own order instead. In every version the student keeps the batch they already own, because we never take access away. So the credit works as a discount, not a transfer.

Pro-rated

Your unused days count

credit = paid × days left ÷ validity
price = list − credit
  • Feels fair, and the student can check the maths
  • Built-in urgency: the credit shrinks every week, with no fake timer
  • Late in the batch, the credit is almost nothing
  • A student who paid more than the target's price would get it free
Existing-user discount

Because you already study with us

price = list − loyalty %
for anyone with an active paid batch
  • One setting covers every targeted batch, with no pairs to configure
  • Easy to say in a push: "25% off for Quicktricks students"
  • No reason to upgrade now rather than later
  • The discount also goes to students who would have paid full price
Recommended

Credit, with loyalty as the floor

price = list − max(credit, loyalty %)
never below the floor price
  • Early upgraders get the big credit, and late ones still get the loyalty discount
  • The floor protects us from students who paid more than the target's price
  • Three dials to set, so the team has to agree on the defaults

Rules the team has to settle

Does the student keep their current batch?Yes. We never take access away, so the credit is a discount we fund. Ending the old batch would only be fair when the target fully contains it.
Which order earns the credit?The paid order on the source batch with the latest expiry, which the upgrade already picks today. Extension orders count at their own price and 183 days.
Two source batches?Use the bigger single credit. Never add credits together.
Free and admin-granted batches₹0 paid earns ₹0 credit and no existing-user discount. They see the list price, or the fixed upgrade price if we keep one.
The price moves every dayCheckout re-checks the price and rejects underpaying. Step the credit weekly so the price a student sees holds for 7 days.
CouponsCoupons already stack on upgrade prices. Apply the floor after the coupon too.
iPhoneApple charges fixed price tiers, so a per-student price can't go through Apple yet. Run the pilot on Android and web.
Extension offer also appliesNo change: the cheaper of the two offers wins, as it does today.

What already exists: the extension offer already sets each student's price from their own order history (50% of their oldest paid order, with a ₹125 floor), and checkout re-checks that price on the server. A pro-rated upgrade is one more price rule in the same place, and the app sees only a different amount.

Proposal

A two-week pilot with no app release

One pair, Railway Maths Foundation Batch to Railway Record Breaking Batch, using only what is live today plus content.

  1. Size the poolRun the read-only offer_pockets.js script to count Foundation holders who don't own Record Breaking, active and recently lapsed.
  2. Turn the offer onSet an upgrade price on Record Breaking Batch with Maths Foundation as the source. Admin form only.
  3. Add the sneak peekTwo free Record Breaking classes, including a short teacher intro, placed as the first topic inside the Foundation batch.
  4. Send one targeted pushPlan broadcast with Advanced audience rows: Active on Foundation, Never on Record Breaking.
  5. Read the resultCount started and paid upgrade orders for the pair after 14 days. That number decides which logic changes we build first.

For the meeting

Questions to settle

Which source → target pairs do we sell first, and who sets each upgrade price?
Pro-rated credit, existing-user discount, or both? And what discount and floor do we start with?
In the pilot, should half the students see flat ₹499 and half the per-student price, so we can compare?
Will Sahil Sir record a 2–4 minute "what comes after Foundation" clip for each pair?
Who keeps the exam calendar, so offer windows open the day a notification drops?
Can someone on the team take mentor calls for an evening slot, and for how many students a day?
Is the prep map worth waiting for the next app release, or should it start as a web page?
What upgrade rate after 14 days would make us build the triggers?
